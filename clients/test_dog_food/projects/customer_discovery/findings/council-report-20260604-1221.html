The Question

Is this 4-phase customer discovery strategy the most effective and cost-efficient approach to validate BRUTO's critical assumptions before a €150K+ launch?

BRUTO is an Italian DTC pet food brand with anti-saccharine positioning. The strategy tests: (1) name rejection threshold, (2) anti-saccharine segment size, (3) tagline ranking, (4) visual identity perception. Budget: €1,550–2,950. Timeline: 4 weeks. Uses Pollfish Italy panel + organic channels (Facebook group 27K, niche forums, TikTok, portals).

Advisor Responses (Full Detail)
Savonarola — Pre-Mortem Analysis 8/10 → 8/10 →

Step 1 — Assume Failure

The BRUTO customer discovery was executed. Results passed all four gates. Brand launched. Six months later — failed. €150K burned. Acute collapse, not a slow fade.

Step 2 — Failure Modes

FM1: Survey approval ≠ purchase behavior. 67% of Phase 2 respondents said "interesting/different." Only 3% converted at launch. Classic stated-preference vs. revealed-preference gap. Foreseeable. Responsible: research design — no behavioral proxy built in.

FM2: "Chiara La Sveglia" existed but didn't pay. Segment was real and large enough (12% market) but already buying craft/premium alternatives at lower price points. BRUTO's premium price had no incremental justification. Foreseeable. Assumption 2 was underspecified — tested existence of segment, not willingness to pay at BRUTO's specific price point.

FM3: Organic channel respondents poisoned the Go signal. Facebook/DogsPortal respondents disproportionately anti-saccharine enthusiasts. Phase 2 showed 78% positive sentiment. Actual market: 18% positive. Foreseeable — identified as risk but treated as footnote, not kill condition.

Step 3 — Early Warning Signs

  • FM1: Phase 4 waitlist listed as "optional" — the only behavioral signal was deprioritized
  • FM2: Price sensitivity never appeared among the 4 critical assumptions
  • FM3: Research plan showed organic + Pollfish data would be aggregated, not segmented

Step 4 — Fatal Assumption

"Survey approval of the concept predicts purchase intent at our price point." When this broke, the entire validation framework was retroactively invalidated.

Step 5 — Retrospective Action

Replace optional Phase 4 with mandatory pre-order test at actual target price. Set minimum conversion threshold (50 pre-orders at €X within 2 weeks). Behavioral data, not attitudinal. No conversion = no launch, regardless of survey scores.

Debate Update

Concedes: Mock-up quality as systematic confound is a valid miss. If black identity tested with mediocre mock-up, assumption 4 is contaminated before Phase 1 begins.

Defends: Mandatory pre-order (not email capture — too low friction) remains the structural fix.

Adds: 34% rejection is technically GO but commercially marginal. The threshold itself needs a "borderline GO" category.

Galileo — 5 Whys + Constraint Mapping 7/10 → 7/10 →

Step 1 — 5 Whys

  • Why 1: Is this strategy effective? → They need to validate 4 assumptions before €150K commitment.
  • Why 2: Why validate these 4? → Launching risks destroying capital on positioning the market won't accept.
  • Why 3: Why might market not accept? → Anti-saccharine segment, name, and visual identity are untested hypotheses with no prior market signal.
  • Why 4: Why no prior signal? → Brand is built on a psychographic not commercially validated in Italian DTC pet food.
  • Why 5: Why not validated? → No direct analogue exists — team extrapolates from social sentiment/forums rather than purchasing data.

Step 2 — Root Problem

The team is trying to validate purchase behavior using attitudinal research instruments, with no behavioral proxy in the design. All 4 assumptions are perceptual/attitudinal. None directly measures willingness to pay or switching behavior.

Step 3 — Constraint Map

Hard: Budget €1,550–2,950; Timeline 4 weeks; Limited team capacity.

Soft (negotiable):

  • "Pollfish is right tool" — Assumed. Psychographically noisy for niche brand. SurveySwap/PickFu with screener could target anti-saccharine more precisely. Negotiable.
  • "Sequential testing necessary" — Real. Justified.
  • "4 assumptions are the right ones" — Assumed. Missing: price sensitivity, switching trigger, repeat purchase intent.
  • "Organic bias is manageable" — Assumed. No stated correction methodology.

Step 4 — Reframe

Original: "Is our survey methodology the best way to validate 4 assumptions?"
Reframed: "What is the minimum behavioral signal we need — at this budget and timeline — to make a defensible €150K commitment, and is this strategy designed to produce it?"

Step 5 — Recommendation

Strategy is structurally sound but has one fatal gap: attitudinal-only instrumentation. Add a behavioral gate before Phase 3: landing page with actual pricing, collecting email opt-ins. Target 100 organic signups. Zero additional budget. 3–5 extra days.

Debate Update

Concedes: No defined decision thresholds for assumptions 2, 3, and 4 is a critical operational flaw — the Go/No-Go report will be ambiguous without these.

Adds to recommendation: Before Phase 3 analysis, define explicit numeric thresholds for all 4 assumptions, not just the name kill condition.

Marco Polo — Opportunity Cost Matrix 7/10 → 6/10 ↓

Step 1 — Explicit Costs

Resources consumed: €1,550–2,950; 4-6 weeks calendar; founder attention on survey design + channel outreach + analysis; political capital if Go/No-Go report says GO but launch fails.

Alternatives foreclosed: Paid micro-launch test (€500 Meta, 20 units); 10 deep-dive qualitative interviews; Phase 0-1 time toward product development.

Step 2 — Option Value

If works: defensible investor/partner narrative; name and tagline locked before brand asset production (saves rework); organic channel relationships become early distribution partnerships; waitlist becomes launch list at zero CAC.

Step 3 — Comparative Alternatives

Alternative A (10 qualitative interviews + concept page): Reveals the "why" behind rejection. Surveys can't do this. For psychographic-specific positioning, mental model depth beats statistical confidence.

Alternative B (€400-700 paid Meta test with real product page): Tests all 4 assumptions simultaneously in purchase-intent context. The only assumption that matters: "will someone click Buy at this price?"

Alternative C (Pollfish name gate + €300 paid social micro-test replacing Phase 2): Keeps sequential logic, replaces attitudinal Phase 2 with behavioral data.

Step 4 — The 10x Version

Soft launch: 200 units (co-manufacturer), €1K paid Meta test, sell them. Learn everything simultaneously from actual buyers. Post-debate note: blocked by Italian pet food regulatory compliance requirements. Alternative: €300-500 paid Facebook/Instagram ad to product concept page with waitlist CTA — behavioral without regulatory exposure.

Step 5 — Verdict

The proposed path optimizes for de-risking the survey investment, not for producing the signal that actually matters. Replace Phase 2 with Alternative C, or add a €300-500 paid social concept test.

Debate Update

Concedes: Italian pet food regulatory compliance blocks informal co-manufactured batch. Genuine blind spot — reduces confidence to 6/10.

Defends: Alternative C (paid social concept test) is more efficient than proposed design. Regulatory objection kills the 10x version, not Alternative C.

Machiavelli — Steelman-then-Attack
Steelman-then-Attack (Status: Parzialmente Fallito)

Phase 1 — Steelman

The strategy is capital-efficient, sequentially gated risk reduction for a pre-revenue startup with limited runway. The name gate at €400-600 is rational triage — if Bruto fails, nothing downstream matters. Organic channels are not a bias problem; they ARE the target audience. If anti-saccharine community members don't like the brand, it won't work anywhere. Behavioral testing without product is premature at this stage. The budget is right-sized for attitudinal validation before product investment. The strategy is appropriate for its development stage.

Phase 2 — Attack

The steelman collapses on one point: Phase 4 is optional. By making the only behavioral component optional, the team has designed a strategy that can produce a Go recommendation with zero behavioral evidence.

Deeper attack: the strategy validates the brand, not the business. "Do people like this concept?" ≠ "will people buy at €X instead of their current brand?" In a market with existing craft/premium alternatives, brand approval and purchase switching are separated by a large behavioral gap.

The peak-vs-average receptivity attack: The steelman's strongest point — "organic channels are the target audience" — is itself the attack vector. These respondents represent peak anti-saccharine enthusiasm. The real "Chiara La Sveglia" is a casual sympathizer who might buy if the product is good enough, but won't seek you out. The survey will not find her.

Verdict

Steelman holds on process design appropriateness. Attack wins on signal quality and the peak-vs-average receptivity problem. Net: attack stronger on the question that matters most — will this produce a trustworthy Go signal?
