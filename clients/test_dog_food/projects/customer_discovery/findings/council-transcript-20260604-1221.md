## ORIGINAL QUESTION

BRUTO is an Italian DTC pet food brand with anti-saccharine positioning. Before committing €150K+ to full launch, the team designed a customer discovery strategy to validate 4 critical assumptions:

1. Kill Condition 3: "Bruto" as a name does NOT generate >35% emotional rejection among target buyers (28-45yo Italian dog owners, online purchasers, urban)
2. The anti-saccharine psychographic segment ("Chiara La Sveglia") is large enough (estimated 10-15% of online pet food market)
3. "Nutri l'Istinto" is the strongest tagline vs 3 alternatives on differentiation + purchase intent
4. The black visual identity is perceived as distinctive (not cheap)

PROPOSED STRATEGY:
- Phase 0 (1-2 weeks): Visual mock-ups + tool setup + channel outreach
- Phase 1 (Week 1): Test name ONLY via Pollfish Italy n=150, €400-600. GO/STOP gate.
- Phase 2 (Weeks 2-3): Full survey on Pollfish n=300 total + organic via Facebook "Alimentazione a 4 zampe" (27K members), Micimiao Forum, @vittoriovet TikTok, DogsPortal.it
- Phase 3 (Week 4): Analysis + Go/No-Go report
- Phase 4 (optional): Waitlist activation

Budget: €1.550-2.950. Timeline: 4 weeks.

Known risks already identified: Pollfish panel psychographic imprecision, organic channel bias, mock-up quality effect, sequential testing time cost, survey does not validate physical product quality.

---

## FRAMED QUESTION

Is this 4-phase customer discovery strategy the most effective and cost-efficient approach to validate BRUTO's critical assumptions before a €150K+ launch? What fatal risks were missed? What would be changed?

## ADVISOR RESPONSES (Initial)

### SAVONAROLA — Pre-Mortem Analysis
**Confidence: 8/10**

**STEP 1 — ASSUME FAILURE.**
The BRUTO customer discovery was executed. Results passed all four gates. Brand launched. Six months later — failed. €150K burned. Acute collapse.

**STEP 2 — FAILURE MODES.**

**Failure Mode 1: Survey approval ≠ purchase behavior.**
67% of Phase 2 respondents said "interesting/different." Only 3% converted at launch. Classic stated-preference vs. revealed-preference gap. Foreseeable. Responsible: research design — no behavioral proxy built in.

**Failure Mode 2: "Chiara La Sveglia" existed but didn't pay.**
The segment was real and large enough (12% market). But they were already buying craft/premium alternatives at lower price points. BRUTO's premium price had no incremental justification. Foreseeable. Responsible: Assumption 2 was underspecified — tested existence, not willingness to pay at BRUTO's specific price point.

**Failure Mode 3: Organic channel respondents poisoned the Go signal.**
Facebook/DogsPortal respondents were disproportionately anti-saccharine enthusiasts. Phase 2 showed 78% positive sentiment. Actual market: 18% positive. Foreseeable — identified as risk but treated as footnote, not kill condition. No correction factor applied.

**STEP 3 — EARLY WARNING SIGNS.**
- FM1: Phase 4 waitlist listed as "optional" — the only behavioral signal, deprioritized.
- FM2: Price sensitivity never appeared among the 4 critical assumptions.
- FM3: Research plan showed organic + Pollfish data aggregated, not segmented separately.

**STEP 4 — FATAL ASSUMPTION.**
"Survey approval of the concept predicts purchase intent at our price point." When this broke, the entire validation framework was invalidated retroactively.

**STEP 5 — RETROSPECTIVE ACTION.**
Replace optional Phase 4 with mandatory pre-order test at actual target price. Set minimum conversion threshold (e.g., 50 pre-orders at €X within 2 weeks). Behavioral data, not attitudinal. No conversion = no launch.

**Key uncertainty:** Whether Italian pet food buyers are more/less susceptible to stated/revealed gap than comparable markets.
**What would change my mind:** Actual conversion rate data from comparable Italian DTC pet food launches showing survey intent correlates with purchase at >60%.

### GALILEO — 5 Whys + Constraint Mapping
**Confidence: 7/10**

**STEP 1 — 5 WHYS.**
- Why 1: Is this strategy effective? → Because they need to validate 4 assumptions before €150K commitment.
- Why 2: Why validate these 4? → Because launching risks destroying capital on positioning the market won't accept.
- Why 3: Why might market not accept it? → Because anti-saccharine segment, name, and visual identity are all untested hypotheses with no prior market signal.
- Why 4: Why no prior signal? → Because the brand is built on a psychographic not yet commercially validated in Italian DTC pet food.
- Why 5: Why not validated? → Because no direct analogue exists — the team extrapolates from behavioral signals (social sentiment, forums) rather than purchasing data.

**STEP 2 — ROOT PROBLEM.**
The team is trying to validate purchase behavior using attitudinal research instruments, with no behavioral proxy in the design. All 4 assumptions are perceptual/attitudinal. None directly measures willingness to pay or switching behavior.

**STEP 3 — CONSTRAINT MAP.**
Hard: Budget €1,550–2,950; Timeline 4 weeks; Team capacity limited.
Soft:
- "Pollfish is right tool" — Assumed. Psychographically noisy for niche. SurveySwap/PickFu with screeners could target anti-saccharine psychographic. Negotiable.
- "Sequential testing necessary" — Real. Justified.
- "4 assumptions are right ones" — Assumed. Missing: price sensitivity, switching trigger, repeat purchase intent.
- "Organic bias is manageable" — Assumed. No stated correction methodology.

**STEP 4 — REFRAME.**
Original: "Is our survey methodology the best way to validate 4 assumptions?"
Reframed: "What is the minimum behavioral signal we need — at this budget/timeline — to make a defensible €150K commitment?"

**STEP 5 — RECOMMENDATION.**
Strategy is structurally sound but has one fatal gap: attitudinal-only instrumentation. Add a behavioral gate before Phase 3: landing page with actual pricing, collecting email opt-ins or soft pre-orders. Target 100 organic signups. Zero additional budget. 3–5 extra days.

**Key uncertainty:** Whether Italian DTC pet food pre-launch can realistically generate 100 organic email signups in 2 weeks with no paid media.
**What would change my mind:** Benchmark data showing Italian DTC brands in comparable niches convert survey interest to waitlist signups at >30%.

## MACHIAVELLI — STEELMAN-THEN-ATTACK

**STEELMAN:** The strategy is capital-efficient, sequentially gated risk reduction. The name gate at €400-600 is rational triage — if Bruto fails, nothing downstream matters. Organic channels are not bias problems — they ARE the target audience. Behavioral testing without product is premature at this stage. The €1,550-2,950 budget is right-sized for attitudinal validation before product investment. The strategy is appropriate for its development stage.

**ATTACK:** The steelman collapses on one point: Phase 4 is optional. By making the only behavioral component optional, the team has designed a strategy that can produce a Go recommendation with zero behavioral evidence.

Deeper attack: the strategy validates the brand, not the business. "Do people like this concept?" ≠ "will people buy at €X instead of their current brand?" In a market with existing craft/premium alternatives, brand approval and purchase switching are separated by a large behavioral gap.

The steelman's strongest point — "organic channels are the target audience" — is the attack vector. These respondents are peak receptivity cases. The real "Chiara La Sveglia" is a casual sympathizer who might buy if good enough but won't seek you out. The survey won't find her.

**Verdict:** Steelman holds on process design appropriateness. Attack wins on signal quality and the peak-vs-average receptivity problem. The optional Phase 4 is the structural failure point.

2. 35% kill threshold never interrogated — 34% rejection is technically GO but commercially marginal
3. No defined success thresholds for assumptions 2, 3, and 4
4. Italian pet food regulatory compliance blocks informal co-manufactured soft launch

### Verdetto di Machiavelli
Steelman holds on stage-appropriateness of attitudinal research. Attack wins on signal quality and the fatal optional status of Phase 4. Net: attack stronger on the question that matters most (will this produce a trustworthy Go signal?).

### La Raccomandazione
Execute the strategy with three specific changes:

1. **Make Phase 4 mandatory.** Landing page with real brand name, tagline, visual, price indicator ("launching at €X — join waitlist"). Target 75 organic signups in 2 weeks before Phase 3 analysis begins. No signups = attitudinal Go is not actionable.

2. **Define numeric thresholds for all 4 assumptions NOW.** Before survey design. Name already has one (>35% rejection = STOP). Define the others: minimum acceptable segment estimate, minimum tagline differentiation score, minimum "distinctive not cheap" rating. Without these, the Go/No-Go report will be interpreted through confirmation bias.

3. **Never aggregate Pollfish and organic channel data.** Report separately. The delta between them is diagnostic — a large gap indicates peak-receptivity inflation and should trigger caution even if both nominally pass.
